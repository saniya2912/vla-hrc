Early Stage · Gateway Report planned Apr–Sep 2026

Project start & Early Stage

Attempted: the project opened with perception-only pilots aimed at a VLM-based human-action monitoring system — GroundingDINO for real-world object detection on a fruit-sorting task, and a pretrained Gemma-3-12B model for both a simulated MuJoCo block-description task and the same real-world monitoring task. In parallel, the pretrained π0.5 policy (trained on a 7-DOF Franka via LIBERO) was deployed zero-shot on a MuJoCo model of Baxter, without any fine-tuning.

What happened: GroundingDINO gave reliable, interpretable object detections but no task-level reasoning. Gemma-3-12B produced accurate free-form scene descriptions but unreliable structured output once more than one spatial relationship had to be verified at once. The zero-shot π0.5 test was functionally wired end-to-end but produced no meaningful task execution: its 7D end-effector actions were naively mapped onto Baxter's 15D joint-velocity space (only the right arm moved), its expected 8D end-effector state instead received raw joint angles, and the gripper command was ignored outright.

Learned / changed: direct transfer of a pretrained VLA policy to a new embodiment is insufficient — either fine-tuning or an intermediate mapping is required. This finding, not the original human-monitoring framing, became the project's actual next step. The Early Stage Report's own research questions (RQ1–4) were still framed around VLM-based monitoring of a human collaborator; that framing was later replaced by VLA embodiment adaptation as the project's centre of gravity.

Evidence: thesis methodology · first commit d9c2f77, 2026-05-07

Baxter simulation & demonstration pipeline

Attempted: a custom 15-DOF Baxter model was built in MuJoCo (dynamic velocity actuators, collision masks, three cameras), together with a scripted demonstration pipeline using damped least-squares inverse kinematics. The task was decomposed into six reusable primitives (red/blue/green × near/far). The first fine-tuning attempt used π0.5's original velocity-control formulation, in which the policy predicts joint velocities.

What happened: velocity control learned to approach the correct block — language grounding worked — but the gripper rarely closed within an episode, so no successful grasp occurred.

Learned / changed: the policy was redesigned to predict target joint positions instead, converted to motion by a proportional controller. This produced the first successful grasping behaviour and became the standard action representation for the rest of the project. By the time of the repository's first tracked commit (2026-05-07), this position-control, six-task setup was already the working baseline.

VLA fine-tuning & policy debugging

Attempted: three successive checkpoints were trained and compared on the same six tasks — pos_run3, then pos_v2 (native 10 Hz demonstration recording, state expanded to 11 dimensions with 3D end-effector position), then pos_v3 (warm-started from pos_v2 with a lower learning rate and additional near-side demonstrations).

What happened: success rose steadily — 20.0% → 40.0% → 43.3% overall — but pos_v3 scored 0/10 on both green-block tasks, which at the time read as a workspace-geometry or joint-limit problem.

Milestone: the Late Stage Gateway Report was submitted around this point (git: c468cea "mres late stage", 2026-06-20). Its own status table marks the simulation environment, demonstration collection, VLA adaptation, the action-representation redesign, and the VLM planner as Completed; planner–policy integration, end-to-end evaluation, and physical deployment as In Progress. Its remaining-work plan named four milestones — policy refinement, planner–policy evaluation, physical deployment, dissertation writing — and did not yet mention cross-embodiment evaluation or a real-data-only fine-tuning pilot; both were added later, as the final stage's new contributions.

Evidence: thesis methodology (planner design) · independently evaluated at 94.6% in the final results

VLM planner

Attempted: the first planner design compared rendered current/goal workspace images directly with Gemma-3-12B.

What happened: swapping which image was labelled "current" versus "goal" produced identical planner output — proof the model wasn't grounding its decisions in the images for this task.

Learned / changed: the planner was switched to the simulator's ground-truth symbolic block state (near/far per block) instead of images, with each block queried independently rather than all three at once — a design motivated directly by the Early Stage finding that Gemma's structured output degraded once multiple similar objects had to be reasoned about together. A closed-loop check-then-replan cycle (max three rounds) was added on top. This version was later evaluated independently across all 56 non-trivial goal configurations, reaching 94.6% exact-match accuracy.

Dataset audit & checkpoint correction

Attempted: an audit of exactly what pos_v3 had trained on, prompted by its complete green-block failure.

What happened: the real cause wasn't geometry at all — the far-side source directory for green demonstrations had been deleted from disk after conversion, so pos_v3 trained on zero green episodes, an effect compounded by pos_v3 being warm-started rather than trained fresh. pos_v4, trained fresh on an audited complete dataset, fixed green (first non-zero results on either green task) but regressed red/blue — it had only 100 demonstrations/task against pos_v3's 230–250, netting a lower overall score (38.3%). Scaling every task to its full data pool via mixture training (v4b run1) then collapsed to 15% despite normal training loss, traced to two independent serving-time normalisation bugs (the wrong normalisation formula at inference, and a one-normalisation-per-session design that broke once one robot served six independently-normalised tasks).

Learned / changed: fixing both bugs and retraining (v4b run2) reached 41.7% overall and the project's best green-far result (70%) — the first checkpoint with a non-zero result on every task. Across this whole sequence, every jump and regression traced to a specific data or infrastructure cause, never to model capacity.

Physical deployment preparation & cross-embodiment start

Attempted: two threads opened in parallel. Physical-robot infrastructure (ROS workspace, deployment scripts, the two-machine control-laptop/lab-PC split, camera-alignment tooling) began going in from 2026-07-25. Two days later, cross-embodiment scenes for Franka Emika Panda and Unitree G1 were being built in MuJoCo, sharing the same table, dividing line, and block layout as Baxter.

What happened: initial scripted demonstration yield across the new embodiments was uneven — Franka collected 128 episodes across the six tasks (green-near yield as low as 3%), G1 113 episodes with two tasks excluded entirely. Both were later re-collected to 693 (Franka) and 699 (G1) episodes across all six tasks after fixing IK and grasp-orientation issues, alongside a corrected evaluation pipeline and sweep results (2026-08-07).

Git: "hrc final experiments started" & "v1 done", 2026-08-14

HRC integration

Attempted: combining the VLM planner and the fine-tuned policy into a working collaborative loop, immediately after v4b: each subtask attempted autonomously, escalated to a human after a fixed retry limit.

What happened: full-pipeline runs initially failed almost completely (0/3, then 0/1 and 0/2 planned subtasks) despite the policy's own validated 30–70% per-task rates — a failure rate improbable enough to demand investigation. Four distinct bugs were found and fixed in sequence: the image-based planner wasn't grounded (fixed by the symbolic-state switch above); the simulation runner fed the policy an 8-dimensional state instead of the 11-dimensional format every other evaluation used; the arm was reset to home only once per session instead of before every subtask; and multi-block scenes placed every block that needed to move away from home simultaneously — a composition absent from every single-task training demonstration.

Learned / changed: the last bug was fixed without retraining, by a rendering-only change: non-target blocks are shown at their home position in the image passed to the policy, while the live simulation and ground-truth state continue using their true position. With all four fixes in place, the escalation mechanism reached 100% final-goal completion across 50 trials, with 46.1% of subtasks requiring human intervention. This remains a working demonstration on representative configurations rather than the larger systematic trial matrix originally proposed.

The Early Stage concept for this stage was a dexterity-based negotiation between human and robot (below); the mechanism actually built is a considerably simpler fixed-retry-count trigger.

Physical robot deployment & real-data fine-tuning

Attempted: the strongest simulation checkpoint (pos_v3, 9/10 in simulation) was deployed directly on the physical Baxter for task 0, with no additional real-world training.

What happened: 0 of 4 trials succeeded. Diagnosis pointed to a physical home pose that differed from the MuJoCo training pose by roughly 28° at the elbow and 12° at the wrist, an unaligned RealSense camera, and sensitivity to language grounding (in-vocabulary "red" behaved more consistently than out-of-vocabulary "brown" for the same block).

Learned / changed: rather than continuing to debug transfer from simulation, a separate real-data-only pilot was run: 28 kinesthetic-teaching demonstrations (1,731 frames) of a simplified push task, fine-tuned fresh from the base π0.5 checkpoint with no simulation data at all. It reached 14/25 (56%) success, with pushing-the-wrong-direction the dominant failure mode — and with the caveat that evaluation positions stayed close to the training distribution, so this measures near-distribution reliability rather than proven generalisation.

Cross-embodiment completion

Attempted: solo checkpoints were trained and evaluated for Franka and G1 on the corrected, re-collected datasets, then compared against a single policy trained jointly across Baxter, Franka, and G1 (each embodiment keeping its own normalisation statistics, combined via OpenPI's mixture-loading support).

What happened: the first solo evaluation round (run1) was later found to have been served through the same family of normalisation bug that caused the v4b run1 collapse, and was re-run (run2) on corrected data. The completed joint-vs-solo comparison showed no uniform transfer effect: Franka improved (34.0%→41.3%, +7.3 pp), G1 regressed slightly (42.0%→34.0%, −8.0 pp), and Baxter regressed sharply (43.3%→10.7%, −32.6 pp). A gripper-engagement diagnostic found the gripper failed to close in 34.7% of Baxter joint-policy trials, against 2.7% (Franka) and 10.7% (G1).

Learned / changed: this thread, like the real-data-only pilot above, was not in the Late Stage Report's remaining-work plan — both became the final stage's own additional contributions, reflected in the thesis's final research questions RQ5 and RQ6.

Git: thesis chapter restructuring, 2026-08-23 – 08-25

Thesis consolidation

The final chapters were restructured and consolidated into the thesis's final form (Introduction, Literature Review, Research Gap and Questions, Methodology, Results, Discussion, Conclusion), folding in all of the work above. The commented-out draft notes left in the thesis source are themselves evidence of this evolution: they record that RQ1–3 (embodiment adaptation, engineering decisions, planner–policy integration) "were established during the earlier stages of the project," while RQ4–6 (escalation-based HRC, cross-embodiment transfer, sim-to-real) "define the new contributions of this final stage."
