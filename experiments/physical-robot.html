Experiment 06 Physical Baxter RealSense Sim-to-real transfer

Physical Robot & Sim-to-Real

Moves the validated simulation pipeline onto the physical Baxter robot: the strongest simulation checkpoint (pos_v3) is deployed directly, with no additional real-world training, to test whether simulation-trained manipulation transfers to physical hardware.

Physical setup with Robot DE NIRO and RealSense camera (faces blurred for privacy).

Network architecture between the robot hardware, the remote control laptop, and the lab PC with GPU.

Overview

Two threads: direct transfer, and real-data-only fine-tuning

This stage has two parts: validating simulation-to-real policy transfer directly, and a separate pilot that fine-tunes a policy from real demonstrations alone rather than continuing to debug transfer from simulation (covered on the Real-Data Fine-Tuning page). Together they test whether the simulation environment built for this project is an adequate proxy for the physical Baxter.

Methodology

Physical setup & sim-to-real transfer

The physical deployment splits computation across two machines, reflecting the physical location of the available sensors and compute: a remote control laptop runs ROS Indigo and the baxter_interface Python 2.7 client library, with direct access to the robot's joint state, gripper state, and its built-in wrist camera over Baxter's native ROS topics, but no GPU suitable for policy inference; a separate, stationary lab PC provides the GPU used for inference and has an Intel RealSense camera attached directly to it for the workspace/scene view. The scene image is captured locally on the lab PC and injected into the observation stream immediately before each inference call. The physical robot uses the same position-control formulation as the simulation policy, with predicted joint-position targets converted to velocity commands using the same proportional controller, velocity limits, and inference frequency used in simulation.

Results

Direct sim-to-real transfer

Physical trials

4

Successful trials

0 / 4

Simulation reference (pos_v3, same task)

9 / 10

Task 0 (move the red block to the far side) — the best-performing task for the pos_v3 checkpoint in simulation at 9/10 — was deployed directly on the physical Baxter for four trials. None completed the grasp-and-relocate task. Analysis identified two main sim-to-real mismatches: the physical home pose differed from the MuJoCo training pose by approximately 28° at the elbow and 12° at the wrist, and the RealSense camera extrinsics had not been numerically aligned with the simulation camera. The trials also showed sensitivity to language grounding, with the in-vocabulary colour “red” producing more consistent gripper closure than the out-of-vocabulary “brown”. While the direct transfer did not succeed, the results point to specific, identified sources of the sim-to-real gap rather than a single unexplained failure — and motivated the real-data-only fine-tuning pilot on the next page.

Failure cases

Sim-to-real gap

Home-pose mismatch

The physical robot's reset pose differed from the MuJoCo training keyframe by ≈28° at the elbow and ≈12° at the wrist — every trial's first observation was already outside the training pose distribution.

Language grounding sensitivity

The in-vocabulary colour word “red” produced more confident, sustained gripper-closure predictions than the out-of-vocabulary “brown” for the same physical block, evidence of language grounding even in a failing trial.
