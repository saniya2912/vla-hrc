Experiment 02 Six-task policy Position vs. velocity control Training & inference pipeline

Checkpoint & Policy Development

The six-task Baxter policy was developed through controlled iterations of the training and demonstration pipeline. Every major jump and regression across eight checkpoints traced to a specific, identified cause in data completeness or serving infrastructure — never to model capacity or optimisation budget.

Overview

From velocity control to a working six-task policy

The first checkpoint, vel_v1, used the velocity-control formulation of π0.5, in which the policy predicts joint velocities. It achieved no successful trials: the gripper rarely closed within the episode. Switching to position control — where the policy predicts target joint positions and a proportional controller converts these into motion — produced the first successful grasping behaviour, and every checkpoint that follows on this page builds on that change.

Methodology

Dataset audit, training & inference pipeline

Two further changes were introduced in pos_v2: demonstrations were recorded at the native 10 Hz control rate rather than downsampled, and the robot state representation was expanded from 8 to 11 dimensions by including the 3D end-effector position. pos_v3 continued training from pos_v2 with a lower learning rate and additional near-side demonstrations. Two deployment-specific train–inference mismatches were also identified and fixed along the way: a gripper-initialisation mismatch, and gripper hysteresis within a single action chunk. The same fix is reused unmodified in the physical-robot deployment.

Gripper initialisation mismatch: without the fix, the reset gripper control value (≈0.65) places the policy near the grasp threshold at step 0, causing spurious early carry-mode behaviour; resetting to fully open (0.0) restores the expected initial state.

Gripper hysteresis: a spurious close–open–close event within a single action chunk would otherwise drop a carried object; a grasp-latch fix prevents this from reaching the gripper controller.

Switching from velocity to position control was the single largest jump in the series (0% → 20% on the intervening pos_run3 checkpoint, not shown above): the velocity-control policy grounded language correctly — it approached the right block — but the gripper stayed open for most of every episode, so no grasp ever occurred. pos_v3's 0/0 on both green tasks looked like a workspace-geometry limit but traced to a data bug: its training set had zero green-block episodes because the far-side source directory had been deleted from disk after conversion. v4b_run2 is the first checkpoint with a non-zero result on every task, and reaches the best green-far result of the project (70%) — at a small, unresolved cost to red-near and blue-far relative to its immediate predecessor.
