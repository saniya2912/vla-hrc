Human-Robot Collaboration

Evaluates whether a human can assist the VLA policy when it is unable to complete an individual subtask, without taking over the entire task. The VLM planner decomposes the overall goal into subtasks; the policy attempts each one autonomously; and after five failed attempts, that specific subtask is handed to a human collaborator.

Human–robot interaction loop for the collaborative task: the robot and the human collaborator exchange visual and verbal instructions while working toward the shared goal configuration.

Overview

Escalating a subtask, not the whole task

The overall task is first decomposed by the VLM planner into a sequence of manipulation subtasks. The VLA policy then attempts each subtask autonomously, with the system tracking its progress against the corresponding goal state. This allows the robot to remain autonomous when it can complete a task successfully, while giving control to the human only when it becomes stuck — escalation is applied at the subtask level rather than requiring the human to take over the entire task.

Methodology

Plan · execute · check · escalate

Each subtask is first attempted autonomously by the VLA policy, with up to five attempts allowed. If it is still unsuccessful after these attempts, it is flagged for human intervention: the human operator completes that specific subtask by repositioning the relevant block, after which the system checks the updated state and continues with the remaining planned subtasks. The current implementation uses a fixed attempt threshold to trigger escalation; it does not determine the cause of failure or allow the human to intervene at an arbitrary point during execution.

Experimental setup

HRC status dashboard

Human Robot Collaboration dashboard with live MuJoCo viewer. It displays the initial and goal configurations, the current block configuration, the VLM-generated task sequence with each subtask marked pending/executing/completed, and the number of attempts made for the active subtask. When the retry limit is reached, the subtask is marked FLAGGED FOR HUMAN; once the human moves the block into position, the dashboard detects the change, clears the flag, and resumes execution automatically.

Over 50 trials with random initial and goal configurations, the VLA completed 48 of 89 subtasks without human intervention (53.9% manipulation success). At the individual-attempt level, only 48 of 282 policy attempts succeeded (17.0% primitive skill success, 3.17 mean retries per subtask) — the gap between these two figures comes from retries: a subtask that fails several times before eventually succeeding still counts once as a manipulation success. Far-side subtasks were markedly more reliable than near-side ones (73.7–81.2% vs. 23.1–37.5% manipulation success across the three colours). Despite the low primitive-level success rate, all 50 trials eventually reached their specified goal configuration through the escalation mechanism, and no trial exhausted the allowed replanning rounds.

Non-target block position ablation

A genuine multi-block rearrangement requires several blocks to be away from their home position at once — a scene composition the policy never saw during single-task training, which caused early full-pipeline trials to fail almost completely. The fix used throughout the results above shows non-target blocks at their home position in the image passed to the policy, while the live simulation and ground-truth state continue to use their true position. A dedicated ablation (blue, near→far, 15 trials per condition, varying the block's true position independently of its rendered position) found that the fix does not recover performance towards the unmodified baseline — it was in fact the worst-performing of the four conditions tested, while rendering the distractors away (without physically moving them) performed similarly to the baseline. Physical distractor position appears to have a larger effect than the rendered position, suggesting a possible reach or collision interaction rather than a purely perceptual effect. This result covers only the blue task with 15 trials per condition and has not been replicated, so it is reported as an observation and open question rather than a confirmed finding.
